Where the data comes from

Three sources feed the tracker. NCSL (the National Conference of State Legislatures) is the primary, most reliable source for enacted cell phone legislation. Ballotpedia is pulled as a supplement when it's reachable — some of its pages sit behind bot-detection that occasionally blocks automated access, so it's treated as a bonus, not a requirement. LegiScan, a legislative-tracking service, supplies everything about bills that are still moving through a state legislature or that recently failed, including the bill's full text when available.

How often it updates

A script runs automatically once a day, pulls fresh data from all three sources above, and rewrites the site's data file if anything changed. This runs on a schedule (GitHub Actions) independent of any one person — nobody needs to manually refresh or edit anything for the tracker to stay current.

What the four categories mean

PermittedState law explicitly allows a software or technology-based approach.
AmbiguousA restriction law exists but doesn't specify a method — could be read either way until a district writes its own policy.
No LawNo statewide legislation. Entirely up to individual districts.
RestrictedA hard, physical-storage mandate (pouches, lockers, etc.) is in place — incompatible with a software-only approach.

How a state gets classified

The script reads the actual bill or summary text for each state and looks for specific language. Words like "pouch," "locker," or "stored off their person" signal a hard physical mandate. Words like "app," "software," or "device management platform" signal a law that's explicitly friendly to a technology approach. When the wording doesn't clearly match either pattern, the state is marked Ambiguous rather than guessed. A small number of states also carry a manually verified correction — used when someone has checked a state's real-world policy against a primary source and confirmed (or fixed) what the automated read produced.

In-progress and failed bills

Any bill still moving through a state legislature, or one that already failed, shows up in that state's detail view with a short, plain-English note on what it would mean for Doorman if it passed. This comes from LegiScan and is kept separate from the enacted-law category above, since a bill in progress hasn't changed anything yet.

A note on accuracy

This tracker is auto-generated from primary legislative sources, not hand-curated, so treat it as a well-informed starting point rather than a legal opinion. If something looks off for a specific state, it's worth checking the bill link directly — and any correction can be added as a permanent, documented override so the fix sticks on every future update.
